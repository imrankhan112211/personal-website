**Short answer:** I cancelled my paid Claude Pro plan about a week into the month, and Claude returned **US$15.33 as account credit** for the days I did not use. ChatGPT gave me nothing back when I cancelled after a similar week. That small refund taught me a big business lesson: treating customers fairly when they leave is one of the cheapest ways to win their loyalty.

## How I moved from ChatGPT to Claude

For the past few months I paid for OpenAI's premium ChatGPT subscription. Honestly, apart from some basic data analysis, I was not getting results with real impact for my work.

Last week, only about 7 days into the billing month, I got frustrated, **cancelled ChatGPT** and switched to **Claude Pro**.

## The free Claude Pro subscription from The Diana Award

After using Claude Pro for 6–7 days, I received a **free one-year Claude Pro subscription** as a recipient of [The Diana Award](https://diana-award.org.uk/). Since the gift now covered my plan, I cancelled the Claude subscription I had been paying for myself.

That is where the interesting part of the story begins.

## What happened when I cancelled Claude Pro

A little while after cancelling, I opened my billing settings and saw that Claude had returned money to my account as credit.

In simple terms:

- I had used the paid plan for **about one week**.
- Claude kept roughly **$5 for that week**.
- The rest, **US$15.33**, came back to my account as credit.

When I cancelled ChatGPT after a similar 7 days, I received **nothing back** for the unused part of the month.

This is exactly where Claude won the game.

## Why a $15 refund creates a thousand-dollar brand impression

As a user, I naturally liked this. It felt fair: I paid for what I used, and nothing more. That one experience made me want to stay with Claude as a **loyal user**, and now I will happily **recommend this AI tool to other people**.

Look at the trade from the company's side:

| What Claude gave up | What Claude got back |
|---|---|
| About $15 in account credit | A customer who trusts the brand |
| | A user likely to stay for the long term |
| | Free word-of-mouth recommendations (like this article) |

By returning $15 as credit, Claude created a brand impression worth far more than $15. A paid advertisement could not have convinced me as strongly as this one fair moment did.

## The business lesson: a refund policy is a marketing strategy

Many businesses see refunds only as lost revenue. My experience shows the opposite. A fair refund policy can be a **strong growth strategy**, because it:

1. **Builds trust.** Customers feel safe paying you when they know they will not lose money on time or value they never received.
2. **Turns ordinary customers into loyal ones.** People remember how you treated them when they left, not just when they paid.
3. **Creates word-of-mouth marketing.** Happy customers tell friends, post about it and recommend you, and that promotion costs you nothing.
4. **Makes customers come back.** A customer who leaves on good terms is far more likely to return later.

### How to apply this in your own business

- **Offer a prorated or partial refund** for unused time on subscriptions, retainers or packages.
- **Make the refund automatic and fast** wherever possible. Claude did not make me ask; the credit simply appeared.
- **Explain it clearly** on your pricing or billing page so customers know you are fair before they buy.
- **Treat cancellation as part of the customer experience**, not the end of it.
