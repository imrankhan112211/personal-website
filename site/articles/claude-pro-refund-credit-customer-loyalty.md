I cancelled my paid Claude Pro plan about a week into the month, and a little later Claude put US$15.33 back into my account as credit for the days I hadn't used. When I cancelled ChatGPT after the same seven days, I got nothing back. That small difference taught me more about keeping customers than most marketing advice I've read.

## Why I left ChatGPT

I had been paying for OpenAI's premium ChatGPT plan for a few months. Honestly, apart from some basic data analysis, it wasn't giving me results that made a real difference to my work. Last week, barely seven days into the billing month, I got fed up, cancelled it and moved to Claude Pro.

## A free year of Claude Pro from The Diana Award

Six or seven days after I started paying for Claude, I received a free one-year Claude Pro subscription as a recipient of [The Diana Award](https://diana-award.org.uk/). Since the gift covered the plan, I cancelled the subscription I had been paying for myself.

That's when the interesting part happened.

I had used the paid plan for about a week. Claude kept roughly $5 for that week and returned the rest, US$15.33, as credit. I didn't ask for it or contact support. It was just there.

ChatGPT had charged me for a full month and, after seven days of use, didn't return anything for the rest of it. This is where Claude won me over.

## Fifteen dollars, and a customer for years

I liked it, and not because of the money. Fifteen dollars won't change my life. What it showed me is that Claude only wanted to be paid for what I actually used. After that, staying with Claude as a regular user feels like the obvious choice, and when people ask me which AI tool to use, this is the one I'll suggest.

Think about it from the company's side. Claude gave back about $15. In return it got a user who trusts it, who will probably stay for years, and who is now writing a blog post about it for free. No ad they could have bought for $15 would have done that.

## What other businesses can learn from this

Most businesses treat refunds as money lost. My experience says a fair refund can work as a growth strategy. A customer who leaves on good terms tells people about it, and often comes back. A customer who feels they paid for something they didn't get also tells people, just not the story you want.

If you run a subscription, a monthly package or a service retainer, it's worth asking whether you could return the unused part when someone cancels, and whether you could do it without making them ask. The cost is usually small. The goodwill lasts much longer.
